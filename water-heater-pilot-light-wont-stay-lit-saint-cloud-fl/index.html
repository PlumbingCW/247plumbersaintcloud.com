Water Heater Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit: A Saint Cloud, FL Guide

A pilot light that will not stay lit leaves a gas water heater unable to make hot water. The causes are few and the first checks are simple, but gas calls for caution, so this guide starts with safety.

Call (321) 463-9969, open 24/7

By the Saint Cloud 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated November 12, 2026.

Safety first

If you smell gas, leave the house at once. Do not use switches or phones inside. From outside, call the gas utility or 911. A plumber comes after the area is safe.

If there is no smell, you can check the basics. Gas water heaters have warnings on the front of the tank. Read them before you touch the controls, and follow the relighting steps on the label.

Electric water heaters have no pilot light, so if you have an electric unit, this article does not apply. Check your breaker instead.

Why a pilot light goes out

The usual causes, from simplest to most involved.

CauseHow it shows upFix
Dirty pilot assemblyWeak or yellow flame, goes out soonClean the pilot and the orifice
Failed thermocoupleLights, then goes out when the button is releasedReplace the thermocouple
Draft or blocked flueGoes out in wind or when the vent is blockedClear the vent, check the draft
Gas supply problemWeak flame or no flameCheck the valve, call the gas utility
Faulty gas control valvePilot lights but main burner will notReplace the control valve
Low gas pressure or a regulator faultPilot goes out on high demandTest pressure, call a pro

A thermocouple is a small sensor that sits in the pilot flame. It tells the gas valve that the flame is present. If it fails, the valve closes and the pilot goes out.

Relighting, in general terms

Follow the instructions on your heater's label, since models differ. The usual order is as follows.

  1. Turn the gas control knob to off and wait five minutes for any gas to clear.
  2. Turn the knob to pilot and hold the pilot button down.
  3. Light the pilot with the built-in igniter or a long lighter, as the label shows.
  4. Hold the button for about a minute after the pilot lights, then release it slowly.
  5. If the pilot stays lit, turn the knob to on and set the temperature.
  6. If it goes out again, stop and call a plumber.

Do not keep trying if the pilot will not stay lit. Repeated attempts can release gas.

When the thermocouple is the problem

A thermocouple wears out from years of heat. A replacement is an inexpensive part, and for a plumber it is a short job. The plumber also checks the pilot assembly, the burner and the flue while the unit is open.

If the unit is old and showing rust, sediment or other problems, a repair may not be the best use of money. Our water heater replacement page explains when replacing is wiser, and the installation page covers choices such as electric or hybrid models.

Electric or gas in a Florida home

Many Florida homes use electric water heaters, and a gas heater needs a gas or propane supply and a proper vent. If your heater is gas and you are replacing it, you can keep gas or switch fuel, depending on what your home supports.

Heat pump hybrid units are popular in warm climates because they work by moving heat from the surrounding air. Ask a plumber which option suits your garage or closet.

Keeping a record for the plumber

A pilot light problem is easier to solve when the plumber knows its history. Note how often the flame goes out, whether it happens after a storm or a gust of wind and whether the burner lights normally when the pilot is on. A flame that dies within seconds suggests a different cause from one that lasts for a day.

Write down the model and the age of the heater from the data plate, and note what color the pilot flame is when it burns. A steady blue flame is a good sign, while a yellow or flickering one calls for professional attention.

Pass those notes along when you call. They shorten the diagnosis and help the plumber decide whether to bring a thermocouple, a gas valve or a replacement unit.

Saint Cloud local note: gas, permits and who to call

Gas availability depends on your address. Ask the gas utility serving your street or your propane supplier whether service is available and who is responsible for the line up to the meter. For problems with gas, call the utility first.

Water heaters are permit-required work on the county permit page, so replacing a gas unit needs one. Swapping a thermocouple is a repair inside the unit that the page does not name, so ask the building department for your address if you are unsure.

Quick answers for Saint Cloud homeowners

Is it dangerous if my pilot light keeps going out?

It can be if gas is escaping. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911. Otherwise, stop relighting after one or two tries and call a plumber.

How do I know if my heater is gas or electric?

A gas heater has a gas line, a control knob with a pilot setting and a flue on top. An electric one has a wiring cable and no flame.

Can I replace a thermocouple myself?

It is a common repair, though gas work carries risk. Plumbing is a regulated trade in Florida, so many homeowners hire a plumber.

Why does my pilot go out when it is windy?

A draft in the flue or a gap near the heater can blow it out. A plumber can check the vent and the combustion air.

Does a water heater repair need a permit in Osceola County?

The county lists water heaters among permit-required work. A part replacement is not named, so ask the building department that covers your address.
