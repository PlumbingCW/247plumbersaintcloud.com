Reclaimed water and your irrigation

Toho Water Authority extends reclaimed water pipes in this part of Osceola County. Whether your lot has reclaimed water is a question for the utility, because it depends on the street and the date of construction. If yours does, the irrigation system is plumbed separately from your drinking water.

The rule that matters is simple. Reclaimed water must never be connected to indoor plumbing or to a hose used for drinking or filling pools. If you are adding an outdoor spigot or an irrigation zone, ask the plumber to confirm which supply it is on. Plumbing installation can handle the connection once you know.

When water is running where it should not

Move quickly and calmly. These steps limit damage while you wait for help.

  1. Close the main water valve at the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to the wet area if outlets or appliances are involved.
  3. Photograph the damage for your records and for any warranty claim.
  4. Call (321) 463-9969 and tell the dispatcher your community, street and what you shut off.

Water meters, shutoffs and a bill that looks wrong

In a new community the first thing to learn is where your shutoff valves are. The main valve is usually at the meter box near the street or on the house side of the meter, and the house also has a valve where the line enters the building. Open and close each one once so you know it moves. A valve that has never been turned can stick.

If a bill looks wrong, read the meter with every fixture off, wait twenty minutes and read it again. A moving dial means water is going somewhere. Irrigation timers, a running toilet and a pool or fountain fill line are common causes, and a plumber can narrow it down with leak detection. Do not assume a high bill is a utility error before you rule out the house.

Permits, codes, license and warranty

Osceola County's permit page lists water heaters, replacement fixtures and re-piping as permit-required plumbing work, and clearing blockages and repairing leaks as not requiring a permit. Ask the office that covers your address. Plumbing in Florida is built to the standards of the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which also governs backflow protection on irrigation connections.

Florida requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed plumber. Check any license at myfloridalicense.com. If your home is new, ask your builder about warranty coverage before you pay for a repair.
