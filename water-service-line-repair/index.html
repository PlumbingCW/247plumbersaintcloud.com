What the water service line is

The water service line is the buried pipe that brings drinking water from the utility's meter to your house. Water service line repair finds a break or leak in that pipe and either patches it or replaces it, often with a new run of pipe.

In Saint Cloud and Kissimmee, the water comes through Toho Water Authority. The utility's meter is the usual dividing line, with the pipe on your side of the meter typically belonging to the homeowner. Confirm where responsibility ends with Toho, because the exact boundary can vary by address.

When a service line leak is an emergency

Call at any hour if water is bubbling up in the yard, if the ground is soft or sinking, if pressure drops across the house or if the meter runs with every fixture closed. We answer 24/7 and book the first open slot, including nights and weekends.

If you can reach the shutoff valve at the meter, close it. If the leak is on the utility's side, call Toho Water Authority. Keep children and pets off soft ground, because water can undermine the soil.

A same-day appointment is offered when available. The plumber confirms the location, shows you the options and states a price before any digging starts.

Signs the service line is the problem

These signs point to the buried line:

  • A wet, soft or sunken patch of yard between the meter and the house, with no sprinkler nearby.
  • Unusually green grass in a strip.
  • Low pressure at every fixture at the same time.
  • A water meter that runs when every fixture is off.
  • The sound of rushing water near the meter or the front wall.
  • A rising water bill with no change in how you use water.

Irrigation zone leaks can look the same, so the plumber checks the sprinkler system as part of the diagnosis.

Repair methods compared

Choose based on pipe condition, soil and what sits on top.

Trenchless replacementPull a new pipe through the old path with two small pitsLong runs under driveways, patios or landscaping

Whatever the method, call 811 before any digging. Sunshine 811 is the free locating service that marks buried utilities, and your plumber will normally coordinate it.

Pipe materials and what they tell you

The age and material of your service line affect the repair. Copper has long been common and can pit or corrode over decades. Polyethylene tubing is flexible and widely used in newer installs. Galvanized steel, found in much older homes, rusts from the inside and chokes down over time. Polybutylene, a gray plastic from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s, can fail at fittings.

If a repair uncovers old pipe in poor condition, the plumber will say so. Patching a corroded line often leads to another leak a few feet away, and replacing the whole run may cost less than repeated digging.

Osceola County's permit page lists re-piping as work that requires a permit and repairing leaks as work that does not, so replacements may call for one. Ask the building department that covers your address.

What decides the price of service line repair

We quote after locating the leak:

  • Length and depth. A short, shallow repair is simpler than a long, deep run.
  • Surface above. Concrete, pavers, trees and sprinklers raise restoration cost.
  • Method. Spot repair, trench and trenchless each have their own price.
  • Pipe material. New pipe is chosen to match the home and local practice.
  • Permit and restoration. Where a permit applies, and patching the yard or driveway afterward.

A sudden break may be partly covered by insurance, though wear and tear usually is not, so ask your agent. To get a price, call (321) 463-9969 and tell us where the wet ground is.

Low water pressure and the service line

Low pressure throughout the house, with no single fixture to blame, can point to a partly failed service line, a closed or failing main valve or a pressure regulator that has worn out. A leak on the service line lowers pressure by letting water escape before it reaches the house.

A plumber starts at the meter, checks the pressure at an outdoor spigot and compares it with a gauge reading, then follows the line. If the cause is a regulator, the fix is small. If it is the line itself, you are into the repair options in this guide.

Who owns which pipe

Toho Water Authority serves Saint Cloud, and the utility generally owns the meter while the house side of it belongs to the homeowner. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing sets the standards for the pipe and shutoff valve on your side, so confirm the boundary with Toho for your address.

Questions we hear from Saint Cloud callers

Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint Cloud?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. Close the meter shutoff if you can reach it, call us and we book the first open appointment.

Who is responsible for the water line, me or the utility?

Typically the utility owns the line up to the meter and the homeowner owns the pipe from the meter to the house. The exact boundary can differ, so ask Toho Water Authority about your address.

How do I find my water meter shutoff?

The meter is in a ground-level box, often near the curb. The shutoff valve is on the house side of the meter. If the box is stuck or full of water, call and we will help.

Can the line be replaced without digging up my driveway?

Often yes, with trenchless methods that need only small pits at each end. The plumber checks the path before recommending it.

Do I need a permit to replace a water service line?

Osceola County lists re-piping as permit-required work and repairing leaks as not. A line replacement may count as re-piping, so ask the building department that covers your address.
