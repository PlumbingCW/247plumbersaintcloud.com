The problems Kissimmee callers raise first

Kissimmee sits on the northwest shore of Lake Tohopekaliga and is the county seat of Osceola County, so its housing stock runs from older downtown blocks to subdivisions and rental homes built in recent decades. The calls reflect that range. Backed-up drains and failed water heaters come first, followed by leaks that nobody noticed in a house that was empty.

Our emergency plumber service for Kissimmee starts with a phone call that works through three questions. Where is the water or the backup? Can you shut it off? What does the house look like, in age and type? Those answers decide what the plumber brings, and they take about two minutes.

Every call is routed the same way. A dispatcher books the first open appointment, and the plumber contacts you with a plan and a clear price before any work starts. After-hours and weekend calls are answered the same as daytime ones.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Kissimmee

Kissimmee's water comes through Toho Water Authority, so the first valve to find is the one at your meter or where the line enters the house. These steps limit damage while you wait.

STEP 1

Shut off the main

Close the main water valve fully, either at the meter box or at the house.

STEP 2

Kill the power to wet areas

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off that breaker and stay out of standing water.

STEP 3

Clear the area

Move electronics, rugs and papers from the floor, and keep children and pets away.

STEP 4

Call the 24/7 line

Call (321) 463-9969, describe where the water is and tell the dispatcher what you shut off.

Vacation rentals and homes that sit empty

Kissimmee has a large number of vacation homes and short-term rentals, and a house that is empty between guests can leak for days before anyone notices. A running toilet, a failed water heater connection or a slow supply line leak can add up to a large water bill and damaged flooring.

If you manage a rental, ask the plumber about shutoff valves at each fixture, braided supply lines and a drain pan under the water heater. A leak sensor on the heater pan or near the main costs little and sends an alert before a small leak spreads. For a hidden leak, leak detection locates it without tearing out the floor.

Turnover days are tight, so tell the dispatcher your check-in time when you call. After-hours service is available so a Sunday-night leak does not close a rental for a week.

Water supply, permits and the Osceola County context

Toho Water Authority's service area page lists Kissimmee, Saint Cloud and Poinciana among the communities it serves. For the Saint Cloud system, the utility's 2025 report describes Floridan aquifer water treated with ion exchange, aeration and chlorine, and it does not list hardness. Kissimmee customers should check Toho's own report for their address and test their tap if scale is a concern.

Osceola County's permit page lists water heaters, replacement fixtures and re-piping as permit-required plumbing work, and clearing sewer blockages and repairing leaks as not requiring a permit. Inside the City of Kissimmee the city building office may run its own process, so ask the office that covers your address.

Plumbing is a regulated trade in Florida, so hire a licensed plumber and check any license at myfloridalicense.com.
