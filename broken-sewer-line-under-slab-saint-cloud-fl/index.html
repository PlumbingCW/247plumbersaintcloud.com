Repair a Broken Sewer Line Under a Slab: A Saint Cloud, FL Guide

A sewer line that runs under a concrete slab is out of sight, and when it breaks, the repair plan has to account for the concrete above it. This guide explains the signs, how the break is found and the repair options.

By the Saint Cloud 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated November 12, 2026.

Why slab sewer problems are different

Most Central Florida homes sit on concrete slabs, and the drain pipes under the slab connect each bathroom, the kitchen and the laundry to the main line. When one of those pipes breaks, wastewater leaks into the soil under the foundation, and the problem can go unnoticed until it affects the house.

The concrete above the pipe is the challenge. Repairs have to reach the pipe from above, from the side or through the pipe itself, and each approach has its own cost and disruption.

Signs of a slab drain break

Watch for these:

  • Drains that clog repeatedly in one part of the house.
  • A sewage smell from the floor, a closet or a wall with no visible leak.
  • Damp or stained flooring, or a floor that feels soft or warm in one spot.
  • A cracked slab or tile with no other explanation.
  • Unexplained gnats, mold or damp carpet near a bathroom.
  • Sewage backing up in a lower fixture when others are used.

These signs overlap with other problems, so a camera inspection is the first step.

How the break is located

The diagnosis uses a camera and a locator.

  1. The plumber runs a camera through the cleanout or a fixture opening.
  2. The camera shows the break, an offset or a collapse, and the distance along the line.
  3. A locator on the camera head lets the plumber mark the surface above the defect.
  4. The plumber measures depth, so the repair can be planned.
  5. You review the footage and the options before any cutting begins.

The footage lets you see the damage, and it supports an insurance claim if one applies.

Pick the method that fits the break, the layout and the budget.

Ask for the method that fits the footage, and ask what finish work comes with it, since floors can be tile, wood or carpet.

What the repair means for your floors

Opening a slab means removing flooring over the break, cutting the concrete, repairing the pipe, backfilling, pouring new concrete and then replacing the floor. Tile and wood may not match perfectly, so ask the plumber what restoration is included.

Lining avoids most of that, which is why it is popular for slab lines. It needs a pipe that can take a liner, and the plumber decides after the camera pass.

A reroute can be less invasive for the floor, though it may require opening walls or running pipe through an attic or a garage. Our sewer inspection page explains how footage guides the decision.

Planning for the days the work takes

A slab drain repair disrupts a household more than most plumbing jobs. The bathrooms and kitchen that share the broken line may be out of use while the work runs, so plan where the family will wash, cook and use a toilet.

Ask the plumber for a sequence before work starts. A typical order is locating the break, opening the floor or choosing a trenchless method, replacing or lining the pipe, testing it with water and then restoring the surface. Each stage has its own timing, and curing or drying times can add to the schedule.

Keep the camera footage and the invoice. Those records help with an insurance conversation, with a later home sale and with any warranty questions on the repaired section.

Quick answers for Saint Cloud homeowners

Will my insurance cover a broken sewer line under the slab?

Coverage varies. Some policies cover accidental discharge and related access costs, and many exclude wear and tear. Ask your agent and keep the footage.

Do I have to break the slab to fix the line?

Not always. Lining, rerouting and tunneling are options that can avoid or limit slab cutting.

How long does a slab sewer repair take?

It depends on the method. A spot repair can take a day or two, and a reroute or a long liner takes longer. The plumber gives an estimate after the camera pass.

Does a slab sewer repair in Saint Cloud need a permit?

Osceola County lists re-piping as permit-required. Ask the building department that covers your address whether your repair counts.

Can a slab drain break cause foundation damage?

Leaking wastewater can wash out soil under the slab over time. If you see cracks, mention them to the plumber.
