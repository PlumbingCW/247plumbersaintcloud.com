An overflowing or leaking toilet can soak a bathroom floor in minutes. The line is open 24/7, so call, then turn the water-supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the flow and lift the tank lid to hold the float up if it is still filling.

What toilet repair and installation cover

Toilet repair fixes the parts that make a toilet flush, fill, seal and sit securely. Toilet installation removes an old unit and sets a new one on a clean flange with a new wax or foam seal and a new supply line.

Most repairs are small. A flapper, a fill valve, a bolt set or a wax ring costs little, and a plumber can often finish the work in an hour or less.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

Call at any hour if the toilet is overflowing and will not stop, if water seeps from the base onto the floor, if the tank is cracked and leaking, or if a clog backs up and the plunger does nothing. The line is answered 24/7, including nights and weekends.

Turn the supply valve on the wall behind the toilet to stop incoming water. If the bowl is rising, take off the tank lid and lift the float or flapper. Stop flushing, put towels at the base and do not use the other toilets if several back up together.

A same-day appointment is offered when available, and a clear price comes first.

Chlorine in tap water slowly attacks rubber. Toho Water Authority's 2025 report lists chlorine as the disinfectant, at an average of 1.57 parts per million, so flappers in Saint Cloud homes tend to age and warp, and a toilet that runs often needs a new one.

How a toilet is replaced

A replacement takes a few hours at most and follows these steps.

  1. Shut off the supply valve, flush the tank empty and disconnect the supply line.
  2. Remove the closet bolts and lift the old toilet away, then scrape the old seal from the flange.
  3. Inspect the flange. A cracked or low flange gets a repair ring or a replacement.
  4. Set a new wax or foam seal, lower the new toilet and tighten the bolts evenly.
  5. Connect the supply line, fill the tank, check for leaks and flush several times.
  6. Caulk the base where appropriate and test for rocking.

The flange is the part most often overlooked. If it sits below the finished floor or is broken, a new toilet will rock or leak no matter how good the seal is.

Choosing a replacement toilet

Look for the EPA WaterSense label, which marks toilets that use 1.28 gallons per flush or less and meet a performance test. A WaterSense toilet can cut water use compared with an older 3.5 gallon unit while still clearing waste.

Check the rough-in, which is the distance from the wall to the center of the flange. The common rough-in is 12 inches, though 10 and 14 inch versions exist. Measuring first avoids a toilet that does not fit.

Height matters for comfort. A comfort-height bowl sits closer to a chair than a standard one, and it suits many adults.

What decides the price of a toilet job

We give a price before work starts, based on:

  • Repair or replace. A flapper or a fill valve is smaller than a new toilet.
  • Toilet model. Standard, comfort height, one piece and elongated bowls differ in cost.
  • Flange and floor. A damaged flange or soft subfloor is extra work.
  • Supply parts. New shutoff valves and braided lines are worth adding to an old toilet.
  • Timing. After-hours service may be priced differently, and we state it up front.

Insurance may cover water damage from a sudden overflow but not the toilet itself. For a price, call (321) 463-9969.

Water efficiency standards

Federal rules cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, and the EPA WaterSense label requires 1.28 gallons or less. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing also sets clearances around the fixture, and Osceola County's permit page lists replacement fixtures as permit-required.

Questions we hear from Saint Cloud callers

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint Cloud?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. Close the supply valve behind the toilet and call, and we book the first open appointment.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A worn flapper that does not seal, or a fill valve that does not shut off, is the usual cause. Both are inexpensive parts, and a plumber can replace them quickly.

How do I know if my toilet needs a new wax ring?

Water around the base, a sewer smell or a toilet that rocks are common signs. A bad seal can leak under the floor without showing, so act when you see any of them.

Can I install a toilet myself?

Many homeowners do, though Osceola County's permit page lists replacement fixtures among the plumbing work that needs a permit, and plumbing is a regulated trade. Ask the building department about your address.

What does WaterSense mean on a toilet?

It is an EPA label for toilets that use 1.28 gallons per flush or less and pass a performance test.
