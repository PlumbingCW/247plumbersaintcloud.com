A community built in the 2000s: what that means for your plumbing

Osceola County first approved the project that became Harmony in October 1992 under the name Birchwood, for up to 7,200 residential units, and the community's Community Development District dates to 2000, with the first residents arriving around 2003. The county rescinded the original development order in 2016, and the land is now regulated as a planned development.

That timeline is useful for plumbing. A home built in the first years of Harmony is more than twenty years old now, which is about the age when original water heaters, supply lines and fixtures begin to fail together. A newer Harmony West home is likely to have little wear but may have builder-grade fixtures.

When you call our emergency plumber line from Harmony, tell the dispatcher the year the home was built. It tells the plumber which parts to bring.

Water heaters from the first homes

Tank water heaters last roughly eight to twelve years under ordinary use, so a heater that came with a house built around 2003 is far past its expected life. If it is still running, it has been lucky. Rust, a puddle at the base or water that no longer gets hot are the usual signs.

A garage or utility closet in a Harmony home is typically on a slab, so a leak spreads across the floor into adjoining rooms. A drain pan with a line to the outside, installed under the tank, buys time. If the heater has failed, see water heater replacement, and for a planned upgrade read water heater installation.

Heat pump hybrid units suit garages with good airflow, and the plumber can check whether your space works.

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Harmony: before we arrive

Harmony sits some distance from Saint Cloud's center, so use the minutes before the plumber arrives well.

STEP 1

Close the main valve

Turn the main water shutoff fully closed. It is usually at the meter box near the curb or where the line enters the home.

STEP 2

Switch off the heater

If the water heater is leaking, close its cold supply valve and turn off its breaker or gas control.

STEP 3

Protect the floor

Move items off the floor and put towels at doors to keep water out of other rooms.
