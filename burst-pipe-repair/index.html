What counts as a burst pipe

A burst pipe is a split, joint failure or break in a pressurized water line that lets water escape faster than a small leak. It may spray from a wall or ceiling, pour from a joint under a sink or appear as a sudden loss of pressure across the house.

Burst pipe repair stops the water, replaces the failed section with sound pipe and checks the rest of the line for the same weakness.

Before the plumber arrives: five steps

Fast action limits damage. These are the five steps in order.

STEP 1

Close the main valve

Find the main shutoff at the meter box or where the line enters the house. Turn it fully closed.

STEP 2

Cut the power

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, turn the breaker off for that area, and do not touch a wet electrical device.

STEP 3

Open a low faucet

Open the lowest tap to drain the line and relieve pressure.

STEP 4

Move belongings

Lift electronics, rugs and papers out of the water.

STEP 5

Take photos

Document the damage and the pipe before cleanup for your insurer.

Why pipes burst in Florida

Freeze bursts are rare in Central Florida but they do happen in hard-freeze winters, mostly on exposed spigots, irrigation lines and attic pipes. If a freeze is forecast, cover or drain outdoor lines and open a tap in an uninsulated space at a slow drip.

Most local bursts have other causes. Corrosion thins a pipe wall until it fails. High water pressure strains old joints and fittings, and a pressure regulator that has failed makes it worse. Settlement and tree roots can crush or shift a line. Water hammer from fast-closing valves slowly loosens connections.

Older polybutylene, a gray plastic pipe used in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid-1990s, is prone to failing at fittings. If your home has it, the repiping page explains what to consider.

After the repair: drying and insurance

Water that sits in drywall and insulation can support mold growth within a day or two, so drying matters as much as the repair. EPA guidance on mold says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Open the area to air, run fans and a dehumidifier, and consider a water damage company if the wet area is large.

Ask your insurer what it covers. Policies commonly cover sudden accidental water damage, including a burst pipe, but often exclude gradual leaks or the cost of repairing the failed pipe itself. Keep photos, receipts and the plumber's invoice.

Cracked, broken and leaking pipe by material

A burst is the dramatic version, but many repair calls start with a smaller failure. A pinhole in a copper line, a cracked fitting on a supply line, a split in a plastic drain pipe and a corroded section of older pipe all call for a repair, and some are urgent because the water has nowhere safe to go.

Copper can be cut out and replaced with a new section, and a repair that joins old and new pipe needs the right fittings. Plastic supply pipe such as PEX or CPVC is replaced in sections as well. A broken drain pipe is different, because it may leak wastewater into a wall or the ground, and a camera or pressure test shows where it has failed.

Standards for the new pipe

Replacement copper, PEX and CPVC pipe are made to ASTM standards, such as ASTM F876 and F877 for PEX, and must be installed under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. A permit question depends on whether the work is a repair or re-piping.

Questions we hear from Saint Cloud callers

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint Cloud?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day, every day. Shut off the main valve and call, and a dispatcher books the first open appointment.

Where is my main water shutoff valve?

It is usually at the meter box near the curb or where the line enters the house, often on the front wall or in the garage. Find it before you need it and test that it closes.

Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Often it covers the water damage but not the repair of the pipe itself, and policies vary. Photograph everything and call your insurer promptly.

Do I need a permit to repair a burst pipe in Osceola County?

Osceola County's permit page lists repairing leaks as not requiring a permit and re-piping as requiring one. Ask the building department that covers your address if the repair expands.

How do I stop water while I wait?

Close the main valve fully, then open the lowest faucet to drain the line. If you cannot find the valve, tell the dispatcher so the plumber knows what to bring.
