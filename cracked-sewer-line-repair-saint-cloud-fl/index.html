Cracked Sewer Line Repair for Saint Cloud, FL Homes

A cracked sewer line rarely announces itself. It starts as a slow drain or a patch of soggy yard and ends as a backup, so this guide covers the signs, the repair options and how to choose.

By the Saint Cloud 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated November 12, 2026.

What a crack in a sewer line does

A sewer line carries wastewater from the house to the city main or septic tank. When the pipe cracks, wastewater leaks into the soil and groundwater and roots enter, which narrows the pipe and invites a blockage.

Cracks have several causes. Age and corrosion weaken older iron and clay pipe. Shifting soil, heavy loads above the line and roots all add stress. In newer plastic pipe, a crack usually follows a crush from construction or poor bedding.

The goal of repair is to restore a sealed, correctly sloped pipe. How you do that depends on the crack.

Signs the line may be cracked

These symptoms often appear before a full backup:

  • Slow drains in several fixtures, or gurgling when water runs.
  • Repeated clogs that return soon after cleaning.
  • A sewage smell in the yard, garage or near a floor drain.
  • A sunken or unusually wet strip of lawn along the sewer path.
  • Lush green grass over the line, because leaking wastewater feeds it.
  • Rodents or insects showing up near drains.

None of these proves a crack on its own, which is why a camera look is the first step.

How the diagnosis goes

A plumber follows this sequence.

  1. Clear the line enough for the camera to pass, if needed.
  2. Run the camera from the cleanout toward the street or septic tank.
  3. Record the footage and mark any defect with a locator.
  4. Measure the distance and depth so the repair can be planned.
  5. Review the footage with you and explain the options.

Ask for a copy of the footage. It helps with insurance and with comparing quotes.

The best choice depends on the crack's length and the pipe's overall condition.

Roots, soil and the Saint Cloud setting

Live oaks and laurel oaks grow well in Central Florida's sandy soil, and their roots follow moisture to leaking pipe joints. A crack and roots feed each other. The crack lets in water, roots grow in and widen the crack, and the cycle continues.

A repair that does not deal with the roots will fail. A plumber may clear the roots with a cable or jet and then seal the defect, and in some cases a section of pipe is rerouted away from a large tree.

Heavy summer rain also affects cracked pipe. Groundwater can enter the pipe through the crack, which adds to the load on the sewer and can cause backups that seem tied to the weather.

Choosing a repair and a plumber

Ask to see the footage that justifies the method. Ask what happens to the rest of the pipe if only one section is replaced. Ask about restoration, since digging a trench through a yard or driveway leaves work behind. Get the price in writing before work starts.

A cleaning visit before the camera may clear a blockage and leave a cracked pipe in place. If you are seeing repeat clogs, sewer line cleaning is a temporary fix, and the camera tells you whether a repair is needed.

Quick answers for Saint Cloud homeowners

Can a cracked sewer line fix itself?

No. A crack lets in roots and water, which tend to make it worse. Repair is the only lasting fix.

How do I know if the crack is in my pipe or the city's?

The homeowner typically owns the line from the house to the property line or the connection point. A camera inspection shows where the defect is, and the utility can tell you where its responsibility begins.

Is pipe lining as strong as a new pipe?

A properly installed liner forms a new pipe inside the old one and can last a long time. It needs a pipe that is still round and in line.

Will insurance pay for a cracked sewer line?

Many policies exclude wear and tear and root damage, though a sudden collapse from a covered event may be treated differently. Ask your agent.

Does a sewer repair in Saint Cloud need a permit?

Osceola County lists re-piping as permit-required and clearing blockages as not. A replacement may need a permit, so ask the building department that covers your address.
